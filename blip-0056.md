## Abstract

This bLIP defines an optional BOLT 12 offer TLV that carries a BIP-340
signature over the offer.

A payer that supports this bLIP can verify that the offer fields it is
using were signed by the private key corresponding to `offer_issuer_id`.

This bLIP provides offer integrity. It does not change BOLT 12 invoice
validation, does not authenticate an issuer identity by itself, and does
not remove the need to validate returned invoices according to BOLT 12.

## Motivation

BOLT 12 offers are not signed. For base BOLT 12 offer fields such as
amount, description, quantity, and expiry, this is usually acceptable:
those fields are copied into the `invoice_request`, copied again into the
`invoice`, and finally authenticated by the BOLT 12 invoice signature.

Offer extension TLVs can have different semantics. Some extension TLVs
affect pre-invoice behavior, such as the endpoint a payer contacts to send
an `invoice_request`. If such a TLV is modified before the payer sees the
offer, the payer may leak an `invoice_request` to an unintended party
before any signed invoice exists to validate.

This bLIP lets offer issuers bind the complete offer TLV stream, including
unknown extension TLVs, to `offer_issuer_id`. It is intended for offers
distributed over channels where the payer may know or later authenticate
the issuer key, but where the offer payload itself may otherwise be copied,
cached, displayed, or relayed by untrusted systems.

## Specification

### Offer TLV

This bLIP defines the following BOLT 12 offer TLV:

1. `tlv_stream`: `offer`
2. types:
    1. type: 1000000057 (`offer_signature`)
    2. data:
        * [`bip340sig`:`sig`]

`sig` is a BIP-340 signature made by the private key corresponding to
`offer_issuer_id`.

Although `offer_signature` carries a signature, it is not a BOLT 12
signature TLV element in the base `240` through `1000` signature range. It
is an offer-extension TLV that reuses the BOLT 12 signature calculation as
specified below.

### Signature Calculation

The writer of a signed offer:

* MUST include `offer_issuer_id`.
* MUST include exactly one `offer_signature` TLV.
* MUST compute `sig` using the BOLT 12 signature calculation with:
  * message name: `offer`.
  * field name: `offer_signature`.
  * signature tag: `lightning` || `offer` || `offer_signature`.
  * signing key: the private key corresponding to `offer_issuer_id`.
* MUST compute the Merkle root over the offer TLV stream after removing
  the `offer_signature` TLV.
* MUST include every remaining offer TLV in the Merkle tree exactly as it
  appears in the offer TLV stream, including unknown extension TLVs.
* MUST use the numerically first remaining TLV as `first-tlv` for BOLT 12
  nonce-leaf calculation.

The reader of a signed offer:

* MUST reject the offer if it contains more than one `offer_signature` TLV.
* MUST reject the offer if it contains `offer_signature` but does not
  contain `offer_issuer_id`.
* MUST reject the offer if `offer_signature` does not parse as exactly one
  BIP-340 signature.
* MUST verify `sig` using the BOLT 12 signature calculation with:
  * message name: `offer`.
  * field name: `offer_signature`.
  * signature tag: `lightning` || `offer` || `offer_signature`.
  * verification key: `offer_issuer_id`.
* MUST compute the Merkle root over the offer TLV stream after removing
  the `offer_signature` TLV.
* MUST include every remaining offer TLV in the Merkle tree exactly as it
  appears in the offer TLV stream, including unknown extension TLVs.
* MUST use the numerically first remaining TLV as `first-tlv` for BOLT 12
  nonce-leaf calculation.
* MUST reject the offer if signature verification fails.

The signature is over the decoded offer TLV stream, not over a particular
ASCII `lno...` encoding. Differences in letter case, continuation markers,
or whitespace that decode to the same offer TLV stream do not change the
signature message.

### Unsigned Offers and Policy

This bLIP does not make all BOLT 12 offers signed.

A reader that supports this bLIP:

* MAY use an unsigned offer if no local policy or higher-level protocol
  requires an offer signature.
* MUST reject an unsigned offer if the context in which the offer was
  received requires a signed offer.
* MUST reject an unsigned offer if another bLIP it is intentionally using
  requires `offer_signature`.

Examples of local policy that may require signed offers include:

* The payer has a saved contact key for the issuer.
* The payer expects offers for a given merchant, service, or endpoint to be
  signed.
* The offer contains extension TLVs that affect pre-invoice behavior.

This bLIP cannot prevent downgrade by signature stripping unless the payer
has a policy or higher-level protocol that requires a signature.

### Copied Fields

When a payer constructs an `invoice_request` from a signed offer, the
`offer_signature` TLV is copied as an offer field according to BOLT 12.

The signature remains a signature over the original offer TLV stream. This
bLIP does not define a new signature over the `invoice_request` or
`invoice` TLV streams. BOLT 12 `invoice_request` and `invoice` signatures
continue to be validated according to BOLT 12.
Readers do not need to re-verify a copied `offer_signature` as a signature
over the `invoice_request` or `invoice`; they validate those objects using
their own BOLT 12 signatures.

## Rationale

This bLIP uses an offer-extension TLV because BOLT 12 currently restricts
offer TLV fields to the base offer range and the offer-extension range.
The TLV type is an arbitrary unused odd type in the BOLT 12
offer-extension range. It is odd because signed offers are optional and
readers that do not understand this bLIP can ignore it; it is not intended
to encode the bLIP number and should not change solely because this bLIP
is renumbered.

The signature covers the complete offer TLV stream, excluding only the
`offer_signature` TLV itself, so unknown future extension TLVs are bound to
the same issuer key. This avoids defining separate signatures for each
extension that needs pre-invoice integrity.

The signature key is `offer_issuer_id` because that is already the BOLT 12
key used by payers to validate returned invoices when the issuer id is
present. This bLIP intentionally does not define signatures for offers that
only contain `offer_paths`, because there is no single unblinded offer key
for readers to use before selecting an onion-message path.

Signed offers provide integrity, not identity. If an attacker replaces the
entire offer with a different signed offer using a different
`offer_issuer_id`, the signature can still verify. Preventing that requires
the payer to obtain or authenticate the expected issuer key through another
mechanism, such as BIP 353, a saved contact, a trusted application context,
or user confirmation.

Related work includes BIP 353, which authenticates DNS-distributed payment
instructions with DNSSEC, the manual exchange in [bLIP 58][], and the
endpoint transports in [bLIP 57][] and [bLIP 59][]. This bLIP is
complementary: BIP 353 can authenticate how an offer is discovered, while
this bLIP lets the offer carry its own integrity check when copied or
redistributed through other channels.

## Privacy and Security Considerations

Signed offers can prevent undetected modification of offer fields, including
extension TLVs that affect where a payer sends an `invoice_request`.

Signed offers do not hide offer contents. Anyone who sees the offer can
read its fields, copy it, and verify the signature.

Signed offers do not authenticate the human, merchant, service, or domain
identity of the issuer. They authenticate only that the offer was signed by
the private key corresponding to `offer_issuer_id`.

If the payer does not know which issuer key to expect, an attacker can
replace the entire offer with a different signed offer. Wallets SHOULD make
the difference between signature validity and issuer identity clear when
showing signed-offer status to users.

If an attacker strips `offer_signature` from a signed offer, supporting
readers can only detect the downgrade when local policy or another bLIP
requires a signed offer. Implementations that rely on signed offers for
contacts, merchants, or endpoint-bearing offers SHOULD persist that policy
and reject later unsigned offers in that context.

Offer signatures do not replace BOLT 12 `invoice_request` or `invoice`
validation. Payers MUST still validate the returned invoice and its BOLT 12
signature before paying.

## Universality

This feature is optional and is not intended to be universal in this bLIP.
BOLT 12 offers without `offer_signature` remain valid BOLT 12 offers unless
another protocol or local policy requires a signed offer.

If signed offers become expected for general-purpose BOLT 12 use, this
feature should be considered for inclusion in BOLT 12.

## Backwards Compatibility

Older BOLT 12 readers that do not understand `offer_signature` can ignore
it because it is an odd offer-extension TLV.

Offers that rely on a signed-offer requirement from another bLIP or local
policy will not be usable through that signed-offer-dependent flow by
wallets that do not implement this bLIP.
